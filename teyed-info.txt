This is a huge dataset and of course there can be some errors in it since I annotated it all alone. I plan to increase the data set in the future and also improve the quality further. So if you find some errors please write me wolfgang.fuhl@uni-tuebingen.de



NvidiaAR and NvidiaVR have an additional "vid_index.txt" file since those are collections of multiple recordings per folder (The split is done automatically and most of it was checked by me but can contain some errors)


Files:
- vid_index: See description above (Only for NvidiaAR and NvidiaVR).
- eye_movements: Error = 1,Blink = 2,Fixation = 3,Saccade = 4,SM = 5

- validity_lid:-1=invalid, 1=valid (In addition you have the inaccuracy measure from the landmarks and the eye movements (FIX,SAC, and SM))
- validity_iris:-1=invalid, 1=valid (In addition you have the inaccuracy measure from the landmarks and the eye movements (FIX,SAC, and SM))
- validity_pupil:-1=invalid, 1=valid (In addition you have the inaccuracy measure from the landmarks and the eye movements (FIX,SAC, and SM))
Note: All landmarks have also an additional average inaccuracy measure for each image. This can be used to remove images where the landmark or the segmentation is not accurate enough. 
	The inaccuracy measure means that higher values are less accurate an is computed with the loss function proposed in:
	@inproceedings{ICMV2019FuhlW,
		author = {Fuhl, W. and Kasneci, E.},
		title = {Learning to validate the quality of detected landmarks},
		booktitle = {International Conference on Machine Vision, ICMV},
		year = {2019},
		month = {11}
	}
	
Why the validity and why providing annotations with inaccuracy value:
	This allows us to also provide pupils during a blink for example which can be interesting for future research
	This allows to simulate a noisy signal for eye movement detection
	To remove the pupils during a blink for example you can use a threshold of <=2.5 or the eye movments (Fixation, Saccade, SM) as validity signal.




- gaze_vec: Normalized gaze vector
- eye_ball: X,Y,Radius (Z is always 0 since it is the origin of our 3D positions)

Computation of the eye ball and gaze vector is based on:
@inproceedings{NNETRA2020,
	author = {Fuhl, W. and Gao, H. and Kasneci, E.},
	title = {Neural networks for optical vector and eye ball parameter estimation},
	booktitle = {ACM Symposium on Eye Tracking Research \& Applications, ETRA 2020},
	year = {2020},
	month = {01},
	publisher = {ACM}
}

- lid_seg_2D.mp4: Segmentation in white, background in black.
- iris_seg_2D.mp4: Segmentation in white, background in black.
- pupil_seg_2D.mp4: Segmentation in white, background in black.

- lid_seg_3D.mp4: Segmentation in the red channel. Depth information in the blue and green channel ((Z/eye_ball_radius)*(255+255)). Blue channel is filled first and afterwards the green channel. On the surface of the sphere.
- iris_seg_3D.mp4: Segmentation in the red channel. Depth information in the blue and green channel ((Z/eye_ball_radius)*(255+255)). Blue channel is filled first and afterwards the green channel. On the surface of the sphere.
- pupil_seg_3D.mp4: Segmentation in the red channel. Depth information in the blue and green channel ((Z/eye_ball_radius)*(255+255)). Blue channel is filled first and afterwards the green channel. On the surface of the sphere.

- iris_seg_3Dplane.mp4: Segmentation in the red channel. Depth information in the blue and green channel ((Z/eye_ball_radius)*(255+255)). Blue channel is filled first and afterwards the green channel. As a disk in the iris plane intersecting the sphere.
- pupil_seg_3Dplane.mp4: Segmentation in the red channel. Depth information in the blue and green channel ((Z/eye_ball_radius)*(255+255)). Blue channel is filled first and afterwards the green channel. As a disk in the iris plane intersecting the sphere.


- lid_lm_2D.txt: Average accuracy and X,Y positions of the landmarks.
- iris_lm_2D.txt: Average accuracy and X,Y positions of the landmarks
- pupil_lm_2D.txt: Average accuracy and X,Y positions of the landmarks

- lid_lm_3D.txt: Average accuracy and X,Y,Z positions of the landmarks. Z is ciomputed based on the eyeball.
- iris_lm_3D.txt: Average accuracy and X,Y,Z positions of the landmarks. Z is ciomputed based on the eyeball.
- pupil_lm_3D.txt: Average accuracy and X,Y,Z positions of the landmarks. Z is ciomputed based on the eyeball.

- iris_eli.txt: OpenCV Rotatedrect parameters.
- pupil_eli.txt: OpenCV Rotatedrect parameters.

- pupil_in_iris_eli.txt: Shape and position adapted to the iris ellipse (OpenCV Rotatedrect parameters).
